From film to a photo on your phone

The app covers the whole journey: from the roll you just shot, to developing at home, to the digital photo. This page uses drawings and real examples to explain what happens at each step.

We start from the negative, not the print

This is the most common misunderstanding. The photo in your album is a copy made by the lab; the original is the strip of film they hand back with the prints (or that you develop yourself).

✓ This is what we photograph
  • It is the original: it holds all the information in the photo.
  • Inverted colours and an orange mask: that is why you need the engine.
  • It keeps better than prints, which fade.
✗ We don't start from this
  • It is a copy: the lab has already chosen colours and exposure.
  • It has less detail in highlights and shadows, and it fades over time.
  • Photographing it is possible (no inversion needed): it could become an extra feature.

The journey in four stages

The app covers every stage after the shot. Chemical development is still essential: an undeveloped roll does not yet hold a visible image, and light would wipe it out.

4  engine ready

Get your photo

The engine removes the orange mask and inverts the colours as a good lab would. Then, if you like, AI cleans it up.

Why "invert colours" is not enough

A colour negative has two "tricks" that a simple invert colours does not know about:

The orange mask. The whole film has an orange tint, even where there is no image (the rebate). If you simply invert, the orange turns into a strong blue cast. The engine measures the exact colour of that rebate and uses it as a reference to neutralise it.

The film's scale. Film records light as "density", a logarithmic scale. The engine inverts in that scale and then simulates print paper, which gives deep blacks and clean whites.

What you need to photograph negatives

An even lightA tablet with a white screen or an LED panel. The app will offer a "light panel mode".
A holderKeeps the film flat: a negative carrier or even a black card with a window cut out.
A steady phoneParallel to the film, ideally on a stand. Shoot RAW if possible.
A dim roomAvoids reflections on the film. And handle the film by the edges only.

Developed film is not damaged by photographing it: light no longer changes it. A roll that is not yet developed, however, must never be opened in the light.

Three versions of the same photo

After development you can choose how much to "help" the image. Each extra step makes the photo prettier but a little less faithful to the film: that is why the versions stay separate and the Original is never touched.

OriginalThe faithful development: the film's colours, with its grain, dust and the noise of the shot.
EnhancedRemoves specks of dust (only isolated ones on light backgrounds) and colour noise, and sharpens slightly. It invents nothing: it only works on the pixels that are there.
AI upscalingA neural network (Real-ESRGAN) enlarges 2 or 4 times and invents plausible pixels and details. Sharper, but those details were not on the film.

Measured on 13 negatives with simulated phone shots: the Enhanced version brings the colours closer to the lab scan (ΔE difference from 7.6 to 6.9) without losing detail. AI upscaling comes out about 3 times sharper than a classic enlargement, but less faithful to the original: it is an aesthetic choice, not a recovery of information. Try it in the tool.

Ready and tested (prototypes)
